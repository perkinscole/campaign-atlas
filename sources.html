Sources and Notes

Where the words, numbers, and pictures on this site come from.

About the quotations

Every journal entry on the map is drawn from a primary source: a diary or letter written during the war, a memoir written afterward by a participant, or an official dispatch or report. Entries are labeled with the author, their role at the time, the date, and the work the words come from. Where a remark was recorded by someone else (for example, Barnard Bee's "stone wall" or Lee's "It is well that war is so terrible"), the entry says who reported it. Ellipses mark places where a passage was shortened. Spelling has been left as the authors wrote it.

About the numbers

Casualty figures combine killed, wounded, captured, and missing. Civil War returns were incomplete and Confederate records especially so; the figures here are the commonly cited estimates from the National Park Service and standard references, rounded. Army strengths are approximate.

About the map

Army routes are simplified lines through the principal towns, fords, and battlefields each army passed, with the date it was there. Real columns spread over many roads and many miles; the line shows where the main body was, not every division. Battle markers are placed at the center of each battlefield and sized by total casualties.
